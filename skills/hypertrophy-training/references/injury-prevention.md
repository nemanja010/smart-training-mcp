## Injury Prevention

### Main Risk Factors

| Factor | Risk | Mitigation |
|--------|------|------------|
| Too rapid progression | High | Max +10% volume/week |
| Insufficient warm-up | Medium | 10-15 min before training |
| Poor technique | High | Prioritize form over load |
| Chronic fatigue | High | Respect deload signals |
| Untreated pain | Very High | Don't train through pain |

### The 10% Rule

- Don't increase volume by more than 10% weekly
- Don't increase load by more than 5-10% after successful set

## Common Injuries

### Shoulder

**Impingement / Rotator Cuff:**
- Symptoms: Pain during overhead, night pain
- Prevention: Face pulls, external rotation, band pull-aparts
- Substitution: Landmine press instead of OHP, floor press instead of bench

### Lower Back

**Disc / Muscle Issues:**
- Symptoms: Pain during flexion/extension, radiation to leg
- Prevention: Core stability, neutral spine, hip hinge technique
- Substitution: Trap bar DL instead of conventional, belt squat

### Knees

**Patellar Tendinopathy:**
- Symptoms: Pain below kneecap, worse on stairs
- Prevention: Eccentric loading, Spanish squats
- Substitution: Leg extension partials, BFR

### Elbows

**Epicondylitis (Tennis/Golfer's Elbow):**
- Symptoms: Pain on inner/outer elbow
- Prevention: Wrist curls/extensions, grip work
- Substitution: Neutral grip, EZ bar instead of straight

## Training Around Injuries

### General Principles

1. **Don't train through acute pain** - worsens condition
2. **Pain-free ROM** - train in range without pain
3. **Reduce load** - 50-70% of normal
4. **Increase frequency** - lighter, more frequent stimulus
5. **BFR** - low load with similar effect

### Substitutions by Problem

| Pain Area | Avoid | Substitute |
|-----------|-------|------------|
| Shoulder (pressing) | Bench, OHP | Floor press, landmine, push-ups |
| Shoulder (pulling) | Pull-ups, rows | Lat pulldown neutral, cable |
| Lower back | Deadlift, squat | Belt squat, leg press, hip thrust |
| Knee | Squat, lunges | Leg extension partial, leg curl |
| Elbow | Curls, extensions | Hammer curl, reverse grip |
| Wrist | Barbell curl, bench | EZ bar, dumbbells, neutral grip |
| Hip | Squat, lunges | Leg press (limited ROM), hip thrust |

### Detailed Substitution Guide by Exercise

**Bench Press alternatives:**
- Floor press (limits ROM, shoulder-friendly)
- Dumbbell press (more natural path)
- Machine chest press
- Push-ups (various angles)
- Cable crossover

**Squat alternatives:**
- Leg press (reduced spinal load)
- Belt squat (zero spinal load)
- Goblet squat (lighter, better mechanics)
- Hack squat
- Split squat (unilateral)

**Deadlift alternatives:**
- Trap bar deadlift (more neutral spine)
- Romanian deadlift (less spinal flexion)
- Hip thrust (glute focus)
- Cable pull-through
- Hyperextension

**Overhead Press alternatives:**
- Landmine press (angled path)
- High incline dumbbell press
- Machine shoulder press
- Lateral raises (if pain-free)

**Row alternatives:**
- Chest-supported row (no spinal load)
- Cable row (adjustable angle)
- Machine row
- Single-arm dumbbell row

**Pull-up alternatives:**
- Lat pulldown (adjustable resistance)
- Assisted pull-ups
- Straight-arm pulldown
- Cable pullovers

## Warm-up Protocols

### General Warm-up (5-10 min)

1. Light cardio (bike, rowing) - 3-5 min
2. Dynamic stretching - 2-3 min
3. Band activation (glutes, rotators) - 2-3 min

### Specific Warm-up (Per Exercise)

**Before heavy compound:**
```
Set 1: Bar only × 10
Set 2: 40% × 8
Set 3: 60% × 5
Set 4: 75% × 3
Set 5: 85% × 1
Working sets: 100%
```

### Mobility Checklist

- [ ] Hip flexor stretch
- [ ] T-spine rotation
- [ ] Shoulder circles
- [ ] Ankle mobility
- [ ] Wrist circles

## Deload and Recovery

### Signals for Deload

| Signal | Severity | Action |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Mild fatigue | Low | Continue, monitor |
| Joint discomfort | Medium | Reduce volume 20% |
| Performance drops 2+ sessions | High | Full deload week |
| Acute pain | Critical | Stop, get evaluated |

### Recovery Protocol After Minor Injury

**Week 1-2:** Complete rest of affected area
**Week 3-4:** Light mobility, isometrics
**Week 5-6:** Progressive return (50% → 75% → 100%)

## When to Seek Professional Help

### Immediately

- Acute trauma (fall, pop)
- Neurological symptoms (tingling, weakness)
- Swelling, redness, fever
- Pain at rest or night pain

### Within a Week

- Pain lasting >7 days
- ROM limitation
- Recurring problem

### Types of Specialists

| Problem | Specialist |
|---------|------------|
| Acute trauma | Orthopedist, ER |
| Chronic pain | Physical therapist, orthopedist |
| Muscle issues | Physical therapist |
| Nutritional recovery support | Registered dietitian |
