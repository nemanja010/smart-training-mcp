## Youth Training (14-18)

### Key Considerations

| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| Safety | Technique-first approach, supervised training |
| Volume | Lower than adults, focus on movement quality |
| Intensity | Moderate loads (60-75% 1RM) |
| Frequency | 2-3x/week full body |
| Growth plates | Avoid maximal loads until skeletal maturity |

### What Works
- Bodyweight exercises and machine-based training
- Learning movement patterns
- Building training habit
- Moderate rep ranges (8-15)

### What to Avoid
- 1RM testing frequently
- Excessive volume
- Training to complete failure
- Advanced techniques (drop sets, forced reps)

## Young Adults (18-35)

### Peak Training Years

This is the optimal period for muscle building:
- Highest natural testosterone
- Best recovery capacity
- Most training tolerance

### Recommendations

| Variable | Guideline |
|----------|-----------|
| Frequency | 4-6x/week |
| Volume | 15-25 sets/muscle/week |
| Intensity | Full spectrum (65-95% 1RM) |
| Recovery | 48-72h between same muscle |

## Masters Athletes (35-50)

### Key Changes

| Factor | Change | Adjustment |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Recovery | Slower | Add extra rest days |
| Hormones | Beginning decline | Optimize sleep, nutrition |
| Joint health | More attention needed | Warm-up longer |
| Injury risk | Increasing | Technique focus |

### Volume Adjustments

- Reduce weekly volume by 10-20% vs. peak years
- Increase warm-up time (15-20 min)
- Consider 4-day splits instead of 6-day

### Training Modifications

- Prioritize compound movements with good technique
- Include more machine work (joint-friendly)
- Add mobility work (10-15 min daily)
- Deload every 4 weeks instead of 6

## Older Adults (50-65)

### Research Summary (Meta-analyses 2020-2023)

- RT produces significant increases in muscle size (ES = 0.34)
- Fiber area increases possible (ES = 0.54)
- Higher volume may be needed for non-responders
- Age negatively associated with hypertrophic response

### Protocol Adjustments

| Variable | Adjustment |
|----------|------------|
| Frequency | 3-4x/week |
| Volume | 12-18 sets/muscle/week |
| Intensity | 60-80% 1RM (avoid very heavy) |
| Rest | 2-3 min between sets |
| RIR | 2-4 (avoid complete failure) |

### Nutrition Adjustments

- **Protein:** 1.8-2.2 g/kg (higher due to anabolic resistance)
- **Leucine:** 3-4g per meal (higher threshold)
- **Vitamin D:** 2000-5000 IU/day
- **Creatine:** 5g/day (particularly beneficial)

### Exercise Selection

**Prioritize:**
- Machines and cables (stability)
- Seated variations
- Unilateral work (balance)

**Limit:**
- Heavy barbell work
- Maximal attempts
- High-impact movements

## Senior Athletes (65+)

### Research Findings

- Very elderly (75+) can still gain muscle and strength
- Muscle size effect size: 0.30 (significant)
- Strength effect size: 0.97 (substantial)
- Higher volume may offset non-responsiveness

### Protocol for 65+

| Variable | Guideline |
|----------|-----------|
| Frequency | 2-3x/week |
| Volume | 8-12 sets/muscle/week |
| Intensity | 50-75% 1RM |
| Rep range | 10-15 reps |
| RIR | 3-4 (avoid failure) |

### Safety Priorities

1. Blood pressure monitoring
2. Proper warm-up (15-20 min)
3. Hydration
4. Rest between sessions (72h minimum)
5. Medical clearance

### Focus Areas

- Functional strength (sit-to-stand, carrying)
- Balance and stability
- Bone density preservation
- Fall prevention
