# Nutrition and Recovery for Runners

## Daily nutrition fundamentals

### Caloric requirements

Running significantly increases energy expenditure. Under-fueling is one of the most common and damaging mistakes runners make.

| Activity level | Approximate daily kcal (70 kg runner) |
|---------------|---------------------------------------|
| Easy run day (45 min) | +250–350 kcal above baseline |
| Hard workout day | +400–600 kcal above baseline |
| Long run day (2 hr) | +600–900 kcal above baseline |
| Base: sedentary adult male | ~2200 kcal/day |

**Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S):**
Chronic under-fueling (common in runners obsessed with weight) causes hormonal disruption, stress fractures, immune suppression, and performance decline. Signs: persistent fatigue, recurring injuries, loss of period in women, low libido, declining performance despite training.

### Macronutrient targets

**Carbohydrates (primary fuel for running):**
- Easy training days: 5–6 g/kg body weight/day
- Hard training days: 7–8 g/kg/day
- Marathon peak weeks: 8–10 g/kg/day
- Sources: oats, rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, fruit

**Protein (muscle repair and adaptation):**
- Minimum: 1.6 g/kg/day for endurance athletes
- Optimal for heavy training: 1.8–2.2 g/kg/day
- Timing: 20–40 g within 30 min post-workout accelerates recovery
- Sources: eggs, chicken, fish, Greek yogurt, legumes, whey protein

**Fat:**
- 20–30% of total calories
- Don't go below 20% — essential for hormones and fat-soluble vitamins
- Focus on unsaturated fats: olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish (omega-3)

## Pre-run fueling

### Before easy/long runs (Z1–Z2)

| Run duration | Strategy |
|-------------|----------|
| <60 min | No pre-run food necessary for most; fasted is fine |
| 60–90 min | Light carb snack 1–2 hr before (banana, toast + jam = 30–50g CHO) |
| >90 min | 60–90g CHO 90–120 min before (oats, rice, or sports drink) |

**Pre-race morning meal:**
- 3 hrs before: 200–300g CHO (porridge/oats, white rice, banana, bagel)
- 30 min before: optional 30g fast CHO (gel, banana)
- Avoid fiber, fat, protein-heavy foods within 3 hr of race start (GI risk)

### Before hard workouts

- Same as long run if >60 min total session
- Caffeine: 3–6 mg/kg body weight 45–60 min before (proven performance benefit)

## During running

### <60 min
Water only. No carbohydrate supplementation needed.

### 60–90 min (including warmup/cooldown)
- 30–45g CHO (1 gel or sports drink)
- Drink to thirst

### >90 min (long runs, marathon)
- **60–90 g CHO/hr** (multiple transportable carbohydrates: glucose + fructose, ratio 2:1)
- Start at 40–45 min; don't wait until hungry
- Train the gut in long runs — GI issues on race day are from poor training of absorption
- Hydration: 400–800 ml/hr depending on heat and sweat rate

### Products and CHO content
| Product | CHO per serving |
|---------|----------------|
| Typical gel | 22–25g |
| Isotonic drink (500 ml) | 30–40g |
| Medjool date (large) | 18g |
| Banana (medium) | 25g |
| Energy chew (1 packet) | 20–25g |

**For 90 g CHO/hr (elite marathon fueling):** 2 gels every 30 min + sports drink at aid stations

## Post-run recovery nutrition

### The recovery window
The first 30–45 minutes post-workout = highest glycogen resynthesis rate (~1.5× normal).

**Recovery meal target:**
- 1.0–1.5 g CHO/kg body weight
- 20–40 g protein
- Within 30–45 min if next workout is within 8 hr

**Practical meals:**
- Chocolate milk (8g protein, 26g CHO per 250 ml) — one of the best bang-for-buck recovery foods
- Greek yogurt + banana
- Rice + eggs
- Protein shake + fruit

### Recovery over 24 hours
- Continue to hit CHO and protein targets
- Sleep is when most recovery hormones are active (growth hormone, IGF-1)
- Anti-inflammatory foods (cherries, turmeric, omega-3s) may reduce DOMS

## Hydration

### Daily baseline
- 2–3 L/day baseline (urine should be pale yellow)
- Add ~500–800 ml/hr of running

### Sweat rate calculation
1. Weigh yourself naked before a 1-hour run (no fluids)
2. Run at race effort with no fluids
3. Weigh yourself after (no toweling off)
4. Sweat rate (L/hr) = pre-weight − post-weight + fluids consumed

Typical: 0.5–1.5 L/hr. Hot/humid conditions can exceed 2 L/hr.

### Electrolytes
Sodium lost in sweat: 400–1500 mg/hr depending on sweat rate and "saltiness."
- Runs <60 min: water only
- Runs >90 min in heat: sodium supplementation (300–600 mg/hr from sports drink or salt tabs)
- Risk of hyponatremia (low sodium): over-drinking plain water in events >4 hr. Drink to thirst, use sodium.

### Signs of dehydration
- Dark yellow urine, infrequent urination
- Headache, dizziness
- HR elevated at same pace
- Performance declining on a workout that should feel manageable

## Sleep

**Minimum target:** 7–8 hr per night; 8–9 hr in heavy training blocks.

**Sleep hygiene for athletes:**
- Consistent sleep/wake time (circadian rhythm)
- Cool, dark room (<19°C improves sleep quality)
- Avoid screens 30–60 min before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin)
- Avoid alcohol (disrupts REM and recovery)
- No caffeine after 14:00 if sensitive

**Sleep extension protocol (research-backed):**
- Adding 90 min sleep per night for 5–7 weeks in athletes improved sprint speed, reaction time, and mood
- Practically: go to bed 45 min earlier; sleep as long as possible

**Naps:**
- 20-min "power nap" before afternoon hard sessions beneficial
- Avoid naps >90 min (sleep inertia)

## Recovery modalities

### Evidence-based (moderate to strong evidence)

| Modality | Benefit | How to use |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Cold water immersion (CWI) | Reduces DOMS, acute inflammation | 10–15 min at 10–15°C within 60 min post-workout |
| Compression garments | Modest DOMS reduction | Wear for 12–24 hr post-long run |
| Sleep extension | Full physiological recovery | Sleep as much as possible in hard weeks |
| Active recovery runs | Clears metabolic waste | 20–30 min very easy Z1 |
| Massage | Reduces subjective soreness | Post-race; not proven for performance |

### Avoid (post-hard workout)
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin) — block inflammation that is part of the adaptation signal. OK for injury; counterproductive for routine recovery
- Ice baths immediately before sleep — disrupts sleep
- Cold therapy after strength training — partially blunts muscle protein synthesis

### Heat therapy
- Sauna (15–30 min at 80–100°C) after easy sessions: plasma volume expansion over weeks (~4–7%)
- Similar benefit to altitude acclimatization for cardiovascular efficiency
- Do not use sauna on hard workout days — adds additional thermal stress

## Weight and running

### Body weight and performance
- ~1% race pace improvement per 1 kg weight loss (simplified)
- BUT: this only applies when weight loss is fat, not muscle or glycogen
- Under-fueling causes muscle loss, hormonal disruption, stress fractures — all counterproductive

### The weight trap
Many amateur runners try to lose weight during base building or peak training. This is the worst time — the body needs fuel to adapt. The only safe time for modest weight loss is early base, low intensity, modest calorie deficit (≤250–300 kcal/day).

### Signs of RED-S
- Fatigue that doesn't respond to rest
- Recurring stress fractures
- Hormonal disruption (lost menstrual cycle in women, low testosterone in men)
- Declining performance despite training
- Low resting HRV, high resting HR
- Immunity issues (getting sick frequently)

## Supplements (what the evidence supports)

| Supplement | Evidence level | Use case |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| Caffeine | Strong | 3–6 mg/kg, 45–60 min before quality sessions or race |
| Creatine | Moderate | Supports recovery, some speed in track events; take 3–5 g/day |
| Beta-alanine | Moderate | Buffers lactate in 1–4 min events (800m–1500m most useful) |
| Sodium bicarbonate | Moderate (uncomfortable) | Acute lactate buffering; GI distress risk |
| Vitamin D | Context-dependent | Supplement if deficient (<50 nmol/L); check annually |
| Iron | Context-dependent | Many endurance athletes are iron-deficient; blood test annually |
| Omega-3 (fish oil) | Moderate | Anti-inflammatory; 2–3 g EPA+DHA/day |
| Nitrates (beet root juice) | Moderate | 500 ml beet juice 2–3 hr before race; improves economy ~1–2% |
| Protein (whey/casein) | Strong for recovery | 20–40 g within 30 min post-workout |

**What not to bother with:** Most "fat burning" supplements, most proprietary blends, exogenous ketones for performance.
