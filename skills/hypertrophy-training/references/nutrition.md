## Protein Requirements

### Meta-Analysis Summary (Morton et al. 2018, updates 2024)

**Optimal Range: 1.6-2.2 g/kg body weight per day**

| Population | Minimum | Optimal | Notes |
|------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Beginners | 1.4 g/kg | 1.6 g/kg | Rapid adaptation |
| Intermediate | 1.6 g/kg | 2.0 g/kg | Standard recommendation |
| Advanced | 1.8 g/kg | 2.2 g/kg | Higher for enhanced MPS |
| Cutting | 2.0 g/kg | 2.4 g/kg | Preserve muscle mass |
| Elderly (>60) | 1.8 g/kg | 2.2 g/kg | Anabolic resistance |

### Protein Distribution

**Per-meal dose: 0.4-0.55 g/kg**
- Example (80kg): 32-44g per meal
- Leucine threshold: ~2.5-3g per meal
- 4-6 meals optimal for maximizing daily MPS

### Protein Timing

| Window | Recommendation | Priority |
|--------|----------------|----------|
| Pre-workout (1-2h) | 20-40g + carbs | Medium |
| Post-workout (0-2h) | 30-50g + carbs | Medium-High |
| Before bed | 30-40g casein/blend | High |
| Upon waking | 30-40g fast-digesting | Medium |

**Note:** Total daily intake > timing specifics

### Protein Sources Ranking (DIAAS)

**Ranked by DIAAS score (Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score):**

1. **Milk** - DIAAS 1.18 (highest digestibility)
2. **Eggs** - DIAAS 1.13
3. **Beef** - DIAAS 1.12
4. **Whey protein** - DIAAS 1.09
5. **Chicken** - DIAAS 1.08
6. **Fish** - DIAAS 1.0+
7. **Soy** - DIAAS 0.90
8. **Pea protein** - DIAAS 0.82

**Note:** Whey protein remains popular despite lower DIAAS due to:
- Fast absorption (ideal post-workout)
- High leucine content (~11%)
- Convenience and mixability
- Cost-effectiveness per gram protein

## Caloric Surplus Strategy

### By Training Status

| Status | Surplus | Weekly gain | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------------|-------|
| Beginner | 300-500 kcal | 0.5% BW | Fast gains possible |
| Intermediate | 200-300 kcal | 0.25-0.5% BW | Moderate approach |
| Advanced | 100-200 kcal | 0.1-0.25% BW | Minimize fat gain |

### Calculating Maintenance

**Methods:**
1. **Bodyweight × 30-33** (rough estimate)
2. **Mifflin-St Jeor + activity factor** (more accurate)
3. **Track 2 weeks** - most accurate

**Activity Multipliers:**
- Sedentary (desk job): 1.2
- Light activity: 1.375
- Moderate (3-4x gym): 1.55
- Very active (5-6x gym): 1.725
- Extremely active (2x/day): 1.9

## Macronutrient Distribution

### Standard Hypertrophy Macros

```
Protein: 1.6-2.2 g/kg (priority)
Fat: 0.7-1.2 g/kg (minimum 20% kcal)
Carbs: Remaining calories
```

### Example: 80kg intermediate (3000 kcal surplus)

| Macro | Amount | Calories | % |
|-------|--------|----------|---|
| Protein | 160g (2.0 g/kg) | 640 kcal | 21% |
| Fat | 80g (1.0 g/kg) | 720 kcal | 24% |
| Carbs | 410g | 1640 kcal | 55% |

## Carbohydrate Strategy

### Training Day Carbs

**Pre-workout (1-3h before):**
- 1-2 g/kg low-GI carbs
- Example: Oatmeal, rice, sweet potato

**Intra-workout (>90min sessions):**
- 30-60g fast carbs/hour
- Dextrose, maltodextrin, sports drinks

**Post-workout:**
- 0.5-1.0 g/kg high-GI carbs
- Helps glycogen replenishment
- Synergy with protein for MPS

### Rest Day Carbs

- Can reduce by 20-30% if needed
- Focus on fiber and micronutrients
- Timing less critical

## Fat Requirements

### Minimum Fat Intake

**Minimum: 0.5 g/kg or 20% total calories**
- Below this: hormonal disruption
- Optimal: 0.7-1.2 g/kg

### Fat Types

| Type | Sources | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Saturated | Meat, dairy, eggs | Limit to <10% kcal |
| Monounsaturated | Olive oil, avocado, nuts | Primary source |
| Polyunsaturated | Fish, flax, walnuts | Omega-3 focus |
| Trans fats | Processed foods | AVOID |

### Omega-3 Requirements

- EPA + DHA: 2-3g/day from fish or supplements
- Anti-inflammatory
- May support muscle protein synthesis

## Hydration

### Daily Water Intake

**Baseline: 35-45 ml/kg body weight**

| Bodyweight | Minimum | With training |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| 60 kg | 2.1 L | 2.5-3.0 L |
| 80 kg | 2.8 L | 3.5-4.0 L |
| 100 kg | 3.5 L | 4.0-5.0 L |

**During training:** 500-1000ml/hour depending on sweat rate

### Signs of Dehydration

- Dark urine (aim for pale yellow)
- Decreased performance
- Cramping
- Headaches

## Meal Frequency

### Research Conclusions

- **3-6 meals per day** - no significant difference for hypertrophy when protein matched
- **4-5 meals** - practical optimum for protein distribution
- Meal frequency impact < total daily intake

## Cutting Phase Adjustments

### Deficit Size

| Goal | Deficit | Weekly loss |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Conservative | 300-500 kcal | 0.25-0.5 kg |
| Moderate | 500-750 kcal | 0.5-0.75 kg |
| Aggressive | 750-1000 kcal | 0.75-1.0 kg |

### Macro Adjustments

- **Protein:** Increase to 2.0-2.4 g/kg
- **Fat:** Maintain minimum 0.5-0.7 g/kg
- **Carbs:** Reduce first, maintain around training

### Preserving Muscle

1. Keep training intensity high (heavy weights)
2. Reduce volume by 30-50%
3. Maintain frequency
4. High protein intake
5. Avoid excessive cardio

## Diet Breaks and Refeeds

### Refeed Days

**Purpose:** Restore leptin, glycogen, performance

**Protocol:**
- Increase calories to maintenance
- Add 50-100% more carbs
- Reduce fat slightly
- Keep protein constant

**Frequency:** Every 7-14 days during cut

### Diet Breaks

**Purpose:** Hormonal and psychological reset

**Protocol:**
- 1-2 weeks at maintenance
- After 8-12 weeks of dieting
- Normal macro distribution
- Maintain training

## Body Recomposition

### Definition

Simultaneous reduction of body fat while maintaining or increasing lean mass, often with minimal change in total body weight.

### Who Can Achieve Recomposition?

| Population | Feasibility | Notes |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Beginners (untrained) | High | "Newbie gains" - most effective period |
| Overweight/Obese | High | Excess fat provides energy buffer |
| Trained returning after layoff | Moderate | Muscle memory effect |
| Trained individuals | Low-Moderate | Requires precise protocols |
| Advanced/Lean athletes | Very Low | Focus on one goal at a time |

### Protocol for Recomposition

**Caloric Approach:**
- Maintenance calories or very slight deficit (-100 to -300 kcal)
- Cycling: Higher calories on training days, lower on rest days
- Example: +200 kcal training days, -300 kcal rest days

**Protein Requirements:**
- Higher than standard: 2.0-2.4 g/kg body weight
- Critical for muscle preservation/growth during deficit

**Training Emphasis:**
- Progressive overload is essential
- Maintain training intensity (heavy weights)
- Moderate volume (avoid excessive fatigue)
- Full body or Upper/Lower splits work well

### Calorie Cycling Example (80kg intermediate)

| Day | Type | Calories | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|-----|------|----------|---------|-------|-----|
| Mon | Training | 2700 | 176g | 320g | 80g |
| Tue | Training | 2700 | 176g | 320g | 80g |
| Wed | Rest | 2200 | 176g | 180g | 75g |
| Thu | Training | 2700 | 176g | 320g | 80g |
| Fri | Rest | 2200 | 176g | 180g | 75g |
| Sat | Training | 2700 | 176g | 320g | 80g |
| Sun | Rest | 2200 | 176g | 180g | 75g |

**Weekly Average:** ~2,485 kcal (slight deficit from 2,600 maintenance)

### Timeline Expectations

- **Beginners:** Visible changes in 8-12 weeks
- **Intermediate:** Slower process, 3-6 months
- **Advanced:** May take 6+ months for measurable changes

### When to Switch to Traditional Bulk/Cut

- Progress stalls for 6+ weeks
- Strength decreasing significantly
- Very lean already (<12% men, <20% women)
- Specific physique deadline approaching

### Key Research

- Barakat et al. (2020): Body recomposition demonstrated in trained individuals
- Frontiers in Nutrition (2024): Research Topic on body recomposition advances
