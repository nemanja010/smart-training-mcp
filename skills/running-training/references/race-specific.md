# Race-Specific Training

## 5K

### Physiological demands
- Duration: 14–35 min (elite → recreational)
- ~95–100% VO2max for the entire race
- Primary limiters: VO2max, running economy, neuromuscular speed

### Key adaptations needed
1. High VO2max (most important)
2. Speed/economy at fast paces
3. Lactate tolerance (not threshold — ability to tolerate fast-accumulating lactate)
4. Efficient pacing strategy (even split or slight negative)

### Training priorities
- 2 quality sessions/week
- Priority 1: VO2max intervals (I pace — 5K race pace)
- Priority 2: Repetitions at R pace (faster than race pace)
- Long run: 60–90 min easy (not the most important session for 5K)
- Weekly mileage: 35–70+ km depending on experience

### Key sessions
- **Classic**: 5 × 1000m at 5K pace, 3 min jog
- **Volume**: 6 × 1200m at 5K pace, 3 min jog
- **Short/fast**: 12 × 400m at faster than 5K pace, 400m jog
- **Speed**: 10 × 200m at mile pace, 200m walk

### Taper
- 10–14 days
- Final hard workout: 10 days out
- Easy + strides in race week

### Race execution
- Start at goal pace (no fast start — burns too much glycogen)
- Km 1: controlled, slightly conservative
- Km 2–3: maintain pace, settle into effort
- Final km: give everything
- The most common mistake: going out too fast in first 400m

## 10K

### Physiological demands
- Duration: 28–60 min
- ~90–95% VO2max
- Lactate threshold is more important than for 5K; speed still critical

### Key adaptations needed
1. High lactate threshold
2. VO2max capacity
3. Aerobic endurance base (more important than for 5K)

### Training priorities
- Priority 1: Threshold/cruise intervals (T pace)
- Priority 2: VO2max intervals (I pace)
- Long run: 90 min+ easy
- Weekly mileage: 45–80+ km

### Key sessions
- **Threshold**: 2 × 15 min at T pace, 2 min recovery
- **VO2max**: 6 × 800m at I pace, 2 min jog
- **Race-specific**: 8 × 1000m at 10K pace, 90 sec recovery
- **Long intervals**: 4 × 2 km at T–I pace, 3 min jog

### Taper
- 14 days
- Final long workout 12 days out
- Short quality run 7–10 days out

### Race execution
- Slightly conservative first km
- Even effort through middle miles
- Final 2 km: build effort
- Pacing error: many runners run the first 5K in 5K effort, blow up the second 5K

## Half Marathon

### Physiological demands
- Duration: 60–120+ min
- ~85–88% VO2max (~marathon pace + 10–15 sec/km)
- Lactate threshold is the primary limiter

### Key adaptations needed
1. High lactate threshold (most important)
2. Aerobic endurance (capacity to run 1–2 hr efficiently)
3. Running economy
4. Glycogen availability (less critical than marathon, but still important)

### Training priorities
- Priority 1: Sustained tempo and cruise intervals
- Priority 2: Long runs with half marathon-pace sections
- Priority 3: VO2max work to drive LT ceiling
- Weekly mileage: 55–90+ km

### Key sessions
- **Extended tempo**: 30–35 min sustained at T pace
- **Cruise intervals**: 5 × 6 min at T pace, 90 sec recovery
- **Long run with race pace**: 18–22 km with 8–10 km at HM pace
- **Progression run**: 14 km progressive from E → HM pace
- **VO2max**: 5 × 1 km at I pace, 3 min jog

### Taper
- 2 weeks
- Week 2 out: 70% volume
- Race week: 40–50% volume, strides

### Race execution
- First 5 km: 5–8 sec/km conservative
- Km 5–15: goal pace
- Final 3 km: negative split attempt
- Nutrition: optional (if >90 min, 1 gel at 45–50 min is beneficial)

## Marathon

### Physiological demands
- Duration: 2:00–6:00+ hours
- ~75–82% VO2max (marathon pace)
- Primary limiters: glycogen depletion, running economy, LT as % of VO2max

### Key adaptations needed
1. Aerobic base — ability to run for 2–4+ hours
2. Fat oxidation efficiency (spares glycogen)
3. Lactate threshold as high % of VO2max
4. Running economy at marathon pace
5. Mental durability (no race-specific training for this — experience only)

### Training priorities
- Priority 1: Long run (20–35 km, with marathon-pace sections)
- Priority 2: Medium-long runs (14–22 km)
- Priority 3: Threshold and marathon-pace work
- Weekly mileage: 65–130+ km

### Marathon pacing strategy (critical)

**Even effort, not even pace.** HR and effort should be constant. Pace will vary with terrain.

**Energy math:**
- Glycogen stores ≈ 2000 kcal = ~32 km at marathon intensity for a 70 kg runner
- Marathon is ~2800 kcal
- Gap must be covered by fat oxidation + exogenous carbohydrate
- Fat oxidation max: 45–60 g/hr at easy pace; only 15–25 g/hr at marathon pace
- Exogenous CHO: 60–90 g/hr required from gel/drink

**Fueling protocol:**
- Start gels at 45 min (don't wait until hungry)
- 60–90g CHO/hr (mix of glucose + fructose for >60g/hr)
- Practiced in long training runs — never new on race day

### Key sessions
- **Long run**: 28–35 km steady Z1–Z2
- **Fast-finish long**: 28 km with last 8–10 km at marathon pace
- **Marathon-pace medium-long**: 18–22 km including 12–15 km at M pace
- **Threshold**: 6–8 km sustained tempo (supports LT ceiling)
- **Progression long**: 30 km negative split (second half faster)

### Taper (3 weeks)
```
3 weeks out: 80% peak volume, 1 quality session
2 weeks out: 60% peak volume, 1 shorter quality session
Race week: 40% volume, easy + strides + 3 km race-pace run on Thursday
```

### Race execution
- Km 1–5: 10–15 sec/km conservative (the crowd pulls you out too fast — resist it)
- Km 5–30: goal pace with even effort
- Km 30–42: hold on; give everything; no reserves left at finish

**Glycogen loading:**
- Days 3–1 before race: 8–10 g CHO/kg body weight per day
- Reduces fat/protein intake to accommodate
- Pasta, rice, bread, sports drinks

**The wall:**
- Occurs when liver glycogen + muscle glycogen depleted (~32 km in undertrained/underfueled runner)
- Prevented by: adequate base mileage, race-pace long runs, consistent in-race fueling
- If it happens: slow down, eat gel immediately, it will partially resolve

## Ultra marathon (50K–100+ miles)

### Physiological demands
- Duration: 3.5–30+ hours
- Well below marathon pace — primarily Z1
- Primary limiters: fueling, hydration, GI tolerance, sleep deprivation, mental durability, terrain adaptation

### Key differences from road marathon
- Running economy matters less (much of ultra is hiking uphills)
- Power hiking is a skill — practice it
- GI tolerance is critical: train the gut with solid food on long runs
- Sleep management for 24+ hr events
- Drop bags and crew strategy

### Training priorities
- Very high volume of easy running (and hiking)
- Back-to-back long runs on Saturday/Sunday (simulate race fatigue)
- Time on feet > pace
- Specific terrain training (hills, trails, if race is on trails)
- Heat training if race is in summer

### Key sessions
- **Back-to-back**: 25 km Saturday + 20 km Sunday (both easy, both with terrain)
- **Very long run**: 35–50 km at ultra effort (never faster than conversational)
- **Back-to-back plus quality**: 25 km with strides on Saturday; 20 km on Sunday

### Race execution
- Start absurdly slow (perceived RPE 2–3/10)
- Eat early, eat often (every 20–30 min from start)
- Walk uphills over 8–10% grade (saves quads for later miles)
- Mental anchor: break the race into aid-station-to-aid-station chunks

## Tune-up race strategies

Use shorter races as quality training sessions in the build phase:

| Goal race | Tune-up race | When | Strategy |
|-----------|-------------|------|---------|
| 10K | 5K | 3–4 weeks out | Race as VO2max session |
| Half marathon | 10K | 4–5 weeks out | 90% effort, treat as quality workout |
| Marathon | Half marathon | 5–6 weeks out | Controlled effort, 85–90% |
| Marathon | 25 km | 4–5 weeks out | Marathon effort for 25 km |

Don't race within 2 weeks of goal event for anything ≥ half marathon.

## Choosing a goal time

Predictor formulas (all approximate, assuming equal training for both distances):

**Riegel's formula:** `T2 = T1 × (D2/D1)^1.06`
- Example: 5K in 25:00 → 10K estimate = 25 × (10/5)^1.06 = 25 × 2.085 = 52:07

**Daniels equivalent race performances:** See VDOT table in `references/zones-and-testing.md`

**Rule of thumb:**
- Half marathon ≈ 5K × 4.65
- Marathon ≈ 5K × 9.5 (well-trained) to × 10 (less marathon-specific training)

**When performance doesn't match the formula:**
- 5K time predicts much faster marathon but marathon is slow → aerobic endurance deficit, not enough long runs or mileage
- Marathon time predicts much faster 5K but 5K is slow → speed deficit, not enough interval/rep work
