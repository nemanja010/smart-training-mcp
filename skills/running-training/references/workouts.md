# Running Workout Library

## Core workout types

---

### Easy run

**Purpose:** Aerobic base development, active recovery, increasing weekly mileage safely.
**Intensity:** Daniels E pace; Z1–Z2 HR; 55–75% CP (Stryd).
**Duration:** 30–90 min.
**Feel:** Can hold a full conversation. Breathing is easy. HR should not drift above Z2.

**Execution:**
- If HR creeps up on hills, slow down — the goal is HR discipline, not pace
- Use easy runs the day before and after hard sessions
- 60–80% of total weekly volume should be easy runs

**Common mistake:** Running easy runs too fast ("gray zone" — too hard to recover, not hard enough to adapt). If in doubt, slow down.

---

### Long run

**Purpose:** Build aerobic endurance, fat oxidation efficiency, mental durability.
**Intensity:** E pace (Daniels); strictly Z1–Z2.
**Duration:** Longest run of the week. Build to 90 min–3.5 hr depending on goal race.

**Guidelines:**
- Should not exceed 30% of weekly mileage for most runners (Pfitzinger uses up to 25–30%)
- Run on tired legs (not the day after a complete rest day) to simulate race fatigue
- Nutrition practice: take gels/carbs starting at 45 min if >90 min
- Effort should feel "sustainably comfortable" throughout

**Variants:**

| Variant | Structure | Purpose |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Steady long | Entire run at E pace | Pure endurance |
| Fast-finish long | E pace → last 20–30% at M or T pace | Racefitness, run-through-fatigue |
| Marathon-pace long | E pace warmup + 60–90 min at M pace + E cooldown | Marathon specificity |
| Progressive long | Negative split: each third faster than the last | Mental strength, aerobic efficiency |

---

### Tempo run (sustained threshold)

**Purpose:** Raise lactate threshold (LT2). The most important quality session for middle-distance to marathon runners.
**Intensity:** Daniels T pace; ~88–92% of LTHR; Stryd Z4.
**Duration:** 20–40 minutes continuous at threshold.
**Feel:** "Comfortably hard" — can say a few words, not a full sentence. Breathing is labored but controlled.

**Structure:**
```
10 min easy warmup
20–40 min at T pace (steady)
10 min easy cooldown
```

**Progression over training block:**
- Week 1: 20 min tempo
- Week 3: 25 min tempo
- Week 5: 30 min tempo
- Week 7: 35–40 min tempo

**Mistake:** Running it too fast. Tempo pace feels controlled. If you're blowing up after 15 min, you're going too hard.

---

### Cruise intervals

**Purpose:** Same physiological effect as tempo but with short recovery breaks — allows more total time at threshold.
**Intensity:** T pace.
**Structure:** 3–5 repetitions of 5–10 min at T pace with 1-minute jog recovery between.

**Examples:**
- 3 × 10 min @ T pace, 1 min jog (total quality: 30 min)
- 5 × 6 min @ T pace, 1 min jog (total quality: 30 min)
- 4 × 8 min @ T pace, 1 min jog (total quality: 32 min)

**When to use:** When you can't maintain a sustained 30+ min tempo, or when adding variety to threshold training.

---

### VO2max intervals

**Purpose:** Push VO2max ceiling. Recruits fast-twitch fibers, improves maximal aerobic capacity.
**Intensity:** Daniels I pace (~5K race pace); 97–100% VO2max; Stryd Z5.
**Feel:** Hard. Breathing is very heavy. Sustainable for ~5 min per rep.

**Standard structures:**

| Session | Reps | Rep length | Recovery | Total quality |
|---------|------|-----------|----------|---------------|
| Classic 5 × 1000m | 5 | 1000m / ~4 min | 3 min jog | 5 km |
| 4 × 1200m | 4 | 1200m / ~5 min | 3–4 min | 4.8 km |
| 8 × 600m | 8 | 600m / ~2.5 min | 2 min | 4.8 km |
| Billat 30/30 | 20–30 reps | 30 sec @ vVO2max | 30 sec jog | 10–15 min quality |
| 6 × 800m | 6 | 800m / ~3 min | 2–3 min | 4.8 km |

**Recovery rule:** Rest = bout duration (work:rest ≈ 1:1). Never race the recovery interval.
**Volume cap:** 8% of weekly mileage maximum for interval work (Daniels rule).

---

### Short intervals / repetitions

**Purpose:** Running economy, neuromuscular power, speed.
**Intensity:** Daniels R pace (mile pace); Stryd Z6–Z7.
**Duration:** Very short — 200m, 300m, 400m.

**Structure:**
- 8–12 × 200m at R pace, full recovery (2–3 min walk/jog)
- 6–8 × 400m at R pace, 400m jog recovery
- 10 × 300m, 200m jog recovery

**Recovery:** Full recovery (unlike VO2max work). The rep should feel powerful and controlled, not desperate. If form breaks down, stop the rep.

---

### Strides

**Purpose:** Neuromuscular activation, leg turnover, running form. Low-stress way to add speed.
**Intensity:** Accelerate to about mile race pace (fast but not all-out) and hold for 15–20 seconds.
**When:** After easy runs or before quality sessions as a warmup. Can be done year-round.

**Structure:**
```
6–10 × 20 sec fast strides on grass or track
Full recovery walk between (45–60 sec)
```

**Technique:** Focus on fast feet and relaxed upper body. Not a sprint; controlled acceleration.

---

### Hill repeats

**Purpose:** Running-specific strength, VO2max stimulus, protects joints vs. flat-ground speed work.
**Intensity:** Z4–Z5; effort equivalent to VO2max intervals.

**Short hills (strength focus):**
- 6–10 × 10–15 sec, steep gradient (8–12%), walk back recovery
- Focus: explosive power, knee drive

**Long hills (VO2max focus):**
- 6–10 × 60–90 sec, moderate gradient (4–6%), jog back recovery
- Focus: sustained hard effort

**Downhill running:**
- 4–6 × 200m controlled downhill at slightly faster than 5K pace
- Builds eccentric quad strength → reduces DOMS in races
- Introduce gradually; high injury risk if too much too soon

---

### Recovery run

**Purpose:** Active recovery — blood flow to clear metabolic waste without adding significant stress.
**Intensity:** Very easy — below Z1. Truly conversational. Stryd <55% CP.
**Duration:** 20–40 min.

**Rule:** If you feel like you need to prove something on this run, you're going too fast. HR should stay 10–15 bpm below your easy run HR.

---

### Double threshold (Norwegian method)

**Purpose:** Maximize time at LT2 per day without accumulated fatigue.
**Structure:** Two sessions in one day — both at lactate threshold, separated by 4–6 hours.

**Example day:**
- AM: 10–15 min warmup + 30 min @ LT pace + cooldown
- PM: 10 min warmup + 20 min @ LT pace + cooldown

**Requirements:**
- Only for athletes with CTL > 70+ and several years of consistent training
- Lactate testing (finger-prick) is used in the original Norwegian protocol to confirm sub-threshold work
- Recovery nutrition between sessions is critical (CHO + protein within 30 min of AM session)

---

### Marathon-pace run

**Purpose:** Neurological and metabolic specificity for marathon pacing.
**Intensity:** Daniels M pace; exactly goal marathon pace.

**Structures:**
- 13–16 km continuous at M pace (medium-long run)
- Long run with 60–90 min at M pace in the middle
- 3 × 5 km at M pace with 2 min rest (early build phase)

---

### Fartlek

**Purpose:** Unstructured speed play — psychologically easier than formal intervals. Good for transitioning from base to quality.
**Structure:** During an easy run, add surges of 30 sec to 3 min at hard effort, with easy recovery between.

**Examples:**
- "1 minute on, 1 minute off" × 10
- Pick-up to a landmark (tree, lamp post) at fast pace, then recover
- Swedish fartlek: 5 min × 3 hard, 4 min × 4 moderate, 3 min × 4 hard, 2 min × 4, 1 min × 4

## Weekly workout combination guidelines

**Two-quality-session week (Daniels standard):**
```
Mon: Rest or recovery run
Tue: Quality 1 (VO2max or repetitions)
Wed: Easy
Thu: Easy + strides
Fri: Quality 2 (tempo or cruise intervals)
Sat: Easy or moderate
Sun: Long run
```

**Three-quality-session week (advanced):**
```
Mon: Rest
Tue: VO2max intervals
Wed: Easy recovery
Thu: Tempo or cruise intervals
Fri: Easy
Sat: Easy + strides
Sun: Long run (can include M-pace or fast-finish)
```

**Rules for sequencing:**
- Never two hard sessions back to back
- Long run is always followed by an easy or rest day
- VO2max sessions demand 48 hr recovery
- Strides and easy runs can be done any day
