## Sleep - The Foundation

### Optimal Sleep Parameters

| Factor | Recommendation | Impact |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| Duration | 7-9 hours | Critical for GH release, MPS |
| Consistency | Same time ±30min | Circadian rhythm optimization |
| Quality | Deep sleep cycles | Hormone regulation |
| Environment | 18-20°C, dark, quiet | Sleep quality |

### Sleep Deprivation Effects

**Acute (<6 hours for 1-2 nights):**
- Decreased testosterone (-10-15%)
- Impaired glucose metabolism
- Reduced performance (strength -5-10%)
- Impaired recovery

**Chronic (<6 hours regularly):**
- Testosterone reduction up to 60%
- Elevated cortisol
- Impaired muscle protein synthesis
- Increased injury risk
- Fat gain tendency

### Sleep Optimization Protocol

**Evening routine (2-3 hours before bed):**
- Dim lights (blue light reduction)
- No caffeine after 2 PM
- Last meal 2-3 hours before bed
- Avoid intense exercise 3+ hours before bed
- Cool room temperature

**Supplements for sleep (if needed):**
| Supplement | Dose | Timing |
|------------|------|--------|
| Magnesium glycinate | 200-400mg | 30-60min before bed |
| Melatonin | 0.5-3mg | 30min before bed |
| Glycine | 3g | Before bed |
| L-theanine | 200mg | 30-60min before bed |

## Active Recovery

### Low-Intensity Cardio

**Parameters:**
- Heart rate: 60-65% HRmax (Zone 2)
- Duration: 20-40 minutes
- Frequency: 2-4 sessions per week

**Benefits:**
- Enhanced blood flow
- Improved nutrient delivery
- Parasympathetic activation
- Reduced muscle stiffness

**Best modalities:**
- Walking
- Light cycling
- Swimming
- Rowing (low resistance)

### Mobility Work

**Daily mobility routine (10-15 min):**
1. Hip flexor stretch
2. Thoracic spine rotation
3. Shoulder circles and stretches
4. Ankle mobility
5. Cat-cow for spine

**When to do:**
- Morning routine
- Pre-training (dynamic)
- Post-training (static)
- Before bed

## Cold Water Immersion (CWI)

### IMPORTANT WARNING

**Research finding (Roberts et al., 2015):**
CWI may significantly IMPAIR hypertrophy when used after resistance training.

**Mechanism:**
- Blunts inflammatory response
- Reduces satellite cell activity
- Impairs signaling for muscle growth

### 2024 Meta-Analysis Update

**Piñero A et al. (2024)** - European Journal of Sport Science meta-analysis (PMID: 38621735)

**Key findings:**
- CWI significantly attenuates long-term muscle hypertrophy
- Effect size: -0.20 (small but meaningful reduction in muscle growth)
- 10-15 studies analyzed with consistent negative effects
- Post-exercise cold exposure blunts mTOR signaling cascade
- Satellite cell proliferation reduced by ~30%

**Practical implications:**
- Confirms Roberts 2015 findings with larger evidence base
- CWI should be actively avoided during hypertrophy phases
- Even "mild" cold exposure (15-18°C) may cause interference
- No safe timing window established for same-day use

**Bottom line:** If muscle growth is your goal, avoid CWI entirely during training blocks.

### When to Avoid CWI

- Within 4-6 hours after resistance training
- During hypertrophy-focused training blocks
- When muscle growth is primary goal
- During any dedicated muscle-building phase (bulk, recomp)

### When CWI May Be Acceptable

- Competition day recovery (acute performance priority)
- During maintenance phases (no growth expected anyway)
- For injury management (acute trauma, not DOMS)
- Endurance-focused training periods
- Off-season/transition periods
- When recovery speed > adaptation is priority

### Alternative Recovery Methods

Instead of CWI for recovery:
- Contrast showers (less aggressive, less interference)
- Active recovery (walking, light cycling)
- Sleep optimization (most powerful recovery tool)
- Nutrition timing (protein + carbs post-workout)
- Light stretching and mobility work
- Massage/foam rolling (no negative effects on hypertrophy)

### CWI References

- Roberts LA et al. (2015). Post-exercise cold water immersion attenuates acute anabolic signalling. PMID: 25953535
- Piñero A et al. (2024). Throwing cold water on muscle growth: A systematic review with meta-analysis of the effects of postexercise cold water immersion on resistance training-induced hypertrophy. Eur J Sport Sci. 24(2):177-189. PMID: 38621735

## Heat Therapy

### Sauna Benefits

**Research-supported effects:**
- Increased growth hormone (acute, not lasting)
- Improved cardiovascular function
- Heat shock protein activation
- Potential muscle preservation during immobilization

### Sauna Protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| Temperature | 80-100°C |
| Duration | 15-20 minutes |
| Frequency | 2-4x per week |
| Timing | Not immediately post-workout |
| Hydration | 500ml water before, 500ml after |

### Contrast Therapy

**Protocol:**
1. Hot (38-40°C): 3-4 minutes
2. Cold (10-15°C): 1 minute
3. Repeat 3-4 cycles
4. End on cold

**Benefits:**
- Blood vessel dilation/constriction cycles
- Improved circulation
- Less aggressive than full cold immersion

## Foam Rolling and Massage

### Foam Rolling

**Evidence:**
- Acute reduction in muscle soreness
- Improved range of motion (temporary)
- No direct impact on hypertrophy
- Psychological relaxation benefit

**Protocol:**
- Duration: 1-2 minutes per muscle group
- Pressure: Moderate (not painful)
- Timing: Pre or post-workout, or on off days

### Sports Massage

**Benefits:**
- Reduced muscle tension
- Improved circulation
- Injury prevention
- Psychological recovery

**Frequency:**
- 1-2x per week if budget allows
- Focus on problem areas
- Not immediately before heavy training

## Stress Management

### Cortisol and Muscle Growth

**Chronic elevated cortisol:**
- Catabolic environment
- Impaired testosterone
- Reduced MPS
- Increased fat storage

### Stress Reduction Strategies

**Physical:**
- Regular exercise (but not excessive)
- Adequate sleep
- Deep breathing exercises
- Yoga or tai chi

**Mental:**
- Meditation (10-20 min daily)
- Nature exposure
- Social connection
- Hobby engagement
- Screen time limits

### HRV Monitoring

**Heart Rate Variability (HRV):**
- Higher HRV = better recovery
- Track morning resting HRV
- Adjust training based on trends

**Interpretation:**
| HRV Trend | Training Adjustment |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Stable/High | Train as planned |
| Slightly reduced | Proceed with caution |
| Significantly reduced | Consider deload |
| Consistently low | Evaluate stress/sleep |

## Deload Week Recovery

### Physical Deload

**Training adjustments:**
- Reduce volume by 50%
- Maintain or slightly reduce intensity
- Focus on technique
- Include more mobility work

### Mental Deload

- Reduce workout duration
- Less tracking/analysis
- Enjoy training without pressure
- Consider outdoor activities

### Nutrition During Deload

- Maintain protein intake (1.6-2.2 g/kg)
- Reduce calories if needed (maintenance)
- Focus on micronutrient-rich foods
- Extra sleep emphasis

## Recovery Between Sessions

### Minimum Recovery Time

| Muscle Group | Recovery Time |
|--------------|---------------|
| Small (biceps, calves) | 24-48 hours |
| Medium (shoulders, arms) | 48 hours |
| Large (back, legs) | 48-72 hours |
| After heavy compounds | 72+ hours |

### Factors Affecting Recovery

**Increases recovery time needed:**
- Training to failure
- High volume
- Novel exercises
- Eccentric emphasis
- Poor sleep
- High stress
- Caloric deficit
- Age (>40)

**Decreases recovery time needed:**
- Moderate RIR (2-4)
- Familiar exercises
- Good nutrition (see [nutrition.md](nutrition.md) for protein timing)
- Quality sleep
- Low external stress
- Caloric surplus
- Experience

## Natural Hormonal Optimization

### Acute Hormonal Response (Not Important)

**Important finding (West & Phillips, McMaster):**
Acute post-workout hormone spikes (testosterone, GH) are NOT necessary for hypertrophy.

**Key points:**
- Local mechanosensitivity > systemic hormones
- Don't optimize training for "hormonal response"
- Focus on progressive overload, not hormone manipulation

### Testosterone Optimization (Natural)

**Lifestyle Factors:**

| Factor | Impact | Recommendation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Sleep | High | 7-9 hours, consistent schedule |
| Body fat | High | 12-20% optimal range (men) |
| Stress | High | Chronic elevation = lower T |
| Exercise | Moderate | Resistance training helps |
| Alcohol | Moderate | Limit to 1-2 drinks, not daily |

**Nutrition for Testosterone:**
- Calories: Don't go too low (prolonged deficit lowers T)
- Fat: Minimum 0.5g/kg (cholesterol is T precursor)
- Zinc: 15-30mg/day (oysters, meat, pumpkin seeds)
- Vitamin D: 2000-5000 IU (if deficient)
- Magnesium: 400-600mg/day

**What Doesn't Work:**
- "Testosterone booster" supplements (tribulus, D-AA, fenugreek)
- Excessive training volume (actually lowers T)
- Extreme dieting (lowers T significantly)

### Growth Hormone (Natural)

**GH is released during:**
- Deep sleep (primary source)
- Intense exercise (acute spike, not anabolic)
- Fasting (minor effect)

**Optimization:**
- Prioritize sleep quality over duration
- Don't eat 2-3 hours before bed
- Keep blood sugar stable
- Avoid alcohol before bed (disrupts sleep architecture)

**Note:** Acute GH spikes from exercise do NOT drive hypertrophy. Focus on training stimulus, not hormone manipulation.

### Cortisol Management

**Chronic high cortisol impairs:**
- Muscle protein synthesis
- Recovery
- Sleep quality
- Fat loss (especially abdominal)

**Reduce cortisol through:**
1. Adequate sleep (7-9 hours)
2. Stress management (meditation, walking in nature)
3. Not overtraining (deload when needed)
4. Social connection
5. Adaptogens (ashwagandha has some evidence)

**Training considerations:**
- Sessions over 90 min elevate cortisol significantly
- Very high volume training increases cortisol
- Deloads help normalize cortisol

### Insulin Sensitivity

**Why it matters for hypertrophy:**
- Better nutrient partitioning (more to muscle, less to fat)
- Enhanced MPS response to protein
- Better recovery

**Improve insulin sensitivity:**
1. Resistance training (primary method)
2. LISS cardio (20-30 min, 3-4x/week)
3. Avoid prolonged sitting
4. Limit refined carbs and sugar
5. Adequate fiber intake (25-35g/day)
6. Adequate sleep
7. Maintain healthy body fat (not too high or too low)

### When to Consider Medical Evaluation

Consult a doctor if:
- Persistent fatigue despite good recovery
- Low libido, mood issues
- Difficulty building muscle despite proper training/nutrition
- Age 35+ with symptoms of low T

**Tests to consider:**
- Total and free testosterone
- SHBG
- Thyroid panel (TSH, T3, T4)
- Vitamin D
- Complete blood count

## Overtraining vs Overreaching

### Functional Overreaching

- Planned temporary performance decrease
- Recovery in 2-3 weeks
- Leads to supercompensation
- Part of periodization strategy

### Non-Functional Overreaching

**Symptoms:**
- Performance stagnation 2+ weeks
- Persistent fatigue
- Sleep disturbances
- Appetite changes
- Mood alterations

**Recovery:** 2-4 weeks reduced training

### Overtraining Syndrome

**Symptoms:**
- All above plus severe
- Resting heart rate changes
- Immune suppression
- Depression
- Extended performance decline

**Recovery:** Months of reduced activity, medical attention

### Prevention

1. Progressive volume increases (not jumps)
2. Regular deloads (every 4-6 weeks)
3. Sleep prioritization
4. Stress management
5. Adequate nutrition
6. Listen to body signals
