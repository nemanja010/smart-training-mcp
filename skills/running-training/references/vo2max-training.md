# VO2max Training — Evidence-Based Guidance

Evidence-backed guidance for prescribing and periodizing VO2max work. Complements the physiology overview in `references/physiology.md` with quantified expectations, dose-response parameters, and the durability/resilience framework.

## What to actually expect from a VO2max block

- **Mean improvement with interval training: ~0.51 L/min** (95% CI 0.43–0.60) — meta-analysis of 37 studies, 334 subjects (Bacon et al., 2013, *PLoS One*).
- Best-case subset (longer intervals): ~0.8–0.9 L/min — this is the upper end, not the average.
- **Absolute scale:** high-intensity interval training (HIIT) produces ~5.5 mL/kg/min; continuous endurance ~4.9 mL/kg/min. Both are "large beneficial effects" (Milanović et al., 2015, *Sports Med*).
- **Less trained = faster gains:** lower baseline fitness yields an additional 1.4–3.2 mL/kg/min (Milanović et al., 2015).
- 6–12 weeks is the window for most measurable gain from a well-run block.

**Key insight:** the "1 L/min" figure sometimes quoted is a best-case ceiling, not an expectation. For an already-fit runner, gains are smaller and harder-won. Slow, steady progress *is* the plan working — set expectations accordingly.

## Intervals vs. easy volume: both work

> "Intervals are the spark. Volume is the fuel."

| Metric | HIIT | Continuous endurance | Difference |
|--------|------|---------------------|------------|
| VO2max gain (mL/kg/min) | 5.5 | 4.9 | +1.2 ("possibly small") |
| Effect magnitude | Likely large | Possibly large | HIIT slightly edges out |

(Milanović et al., 2015 — 28 studies, 723 participants.)

Nuance:

- HIIT is slightly more effective, but the difference is "possibly small" — **both produce large improvements**.
- Longer HIIT repetitions produce *less* additional improvement (−1.8 mL/kg/min), suggesting shorter intervals may be more efficient.
- Longer interventions (>4 weeks) and lower baseline fitness amplify the HIIT advantage.

**Implication:** intensity is not the magic — the total work is. Use hard intervals to drive the ceiling up and easy volume to sustain it. Consistency over years beats any single workout type.

## Dose-response parameters (verified)

From Yang et al., 2025 (*BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil*) — network meta-analysis of 51 studies, 1,261 athletes (PROSPERO CRD42023435021):

- **Repeated sprint training (RST)** 3×/week × 2 weeks is sufficient to produce improvement.
- **Effect-size ranking:** RST (g=1.04) > HIIT (g=1.01) > SIT (g=0.69) > continuous (g=0.29). No significant difference between RST, HIIT, and SIT.
- **HIIT optimum:** ~140 s work bouts; work-to-recovery ratio ~0.85 (inverted U — 140 s is the sweet spot).
- **SIT parameters:** sprints ≤30 s, recovery <97 s (effectiveness drops when recovery exceeds 97 s).
- **Overall:** 3–6 weeks of running-based HIIT (140 s work, ~165 s recovery) or SIT (≤30 s, <97 s recovery) at 3 sessions/week.

Three evidence-aligned session templates:

| Type | Protocol | RPE | Effort |
|------|----------|-----|--------|
| A: VO2 intervals | 5–6 × 140 s hard, 195 s easy recovery | 8–9 | 5K race effort |
| B: Short sprints | 8–10 × 30 s fast, ≤97 s recovery | 9 | mile–1500 m effort |
| C: Repeated sprints | 2–3 sets of 6–8 × 10 s all-out, 90 s between reps, 2–3 min between sets | 10 | all-out |

The 195 s recovery in template A is more conservative than the 0.85 WRR optimum (~165 s) — safer for recreational athletes.

## The ~10-week plateau

- Polarized training is superior for VO2max **only in interventions <12 weeks** (Silva Oliveira et al., 2023) — beyond that the advantage disappears, suggesting a ceiling.
- Optimal intervention duration for HIIT/SIT is **3–6 weeks**; the inverted-U dose-response for work duration and training weeks indicates diminishing returns after ~6–10 weeks (Yang et al., 2025).

**Implication:** a dedicated VO2max block should run **6–10 weeks**, then rotate stimulus (shift to threshold, increase volume, or change intensity distribution). Hammering VO2max intervals past this yields diminishing returns and raises injury risk.

## Durability / physiological resilience

VO2max is a variable, not a fixed score — it degrades during long efforts. Two key papers:

- **Maunder et al., 2021 (*Sports Med*):** defines **durability** as "the time of onset and magnitude of deterioration in physiological-profiling characteristics over time during prolonged exercise." Resting profiles (VO2max, thresholds, economy) degrade during prolonged exercise, with large inter-individual variability. Static profiling therefore overestimates sustainable performance.
- **Jones & Kirby, 2025 (*Scand J Med Sci Sports*):** adds **physiological resilience** to the Joyner model:
  `Performance = VO2max × Lactate threshold × Running economy × Resilience`

Three training strategies build resilience:

1. **Consistency + large volumes over years** — the "boring consistency" that underpins durability.
2. **Prolonged sessions with high-intensity bouts at race pace or above**, especially when already fatigued.
3. **Resistance training — heavy strength + plyometrics** — positive effects on resilience.

**Implication:** a high lab VO2max does not guarantee a fast marathon. What matters is how much of that VO2max can be *sustained* after 2+ hours. The training that builds durability (fatigue resistance) differs from the training that builds peak VO2max (ceiling height).

## VO2max is not the best predictor of performance

- VO2max is limited primarily by the cardiorespiratory system's ability to **deliver oxygen** (Bassett & Howley, 2000, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* — foundational, 4,200+ citations).
- Among athletes with similar VO2max, the one with the higher lactate threshold + better economy (+ resilience) wins.
- Train the other factors, not just the ceiling.

## Practical take-home

1. VO2max gains are real but slow — expect ~0.4–0.6 L/min, not 1 L/min.
2. A 2-week RST kickstart (3×/week) is evidence-backed.
3. Intervals AND easy volume both matter — use both.
4. Rotate stimulus after ~6–10 weeks of dedicated VO2max work.
5. Durability is trainable — lift heavy, practice race pace while fatigued, bank years of volume.
6. VO2max ≠ performance — threshold, economy, and durability all matter independently.
7. Polarized (80/20) is supported, but mostly for trained athletes in blocks <12 weeks.
8. Think in years, not weeks.
