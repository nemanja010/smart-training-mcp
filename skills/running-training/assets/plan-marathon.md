# Marathon Training Plan

## How to use

1. Establish VDOT from recent half marathon or 10K race (`scripts/vdot.py`)
2. Marathon pace (M pace) = Daniels' M pace for your VDOT
3. All easy runs are genuinely easy — never more than 75% LTHR
4. The long run is the cornerstone session; protect it above all others
5. Fuel every long run >90 min (60–90g CHO/hr starting at 40–45 min)

**Prerequisite:** 12+ months of consistent running, comfortable at 65+ km/week, have run a half marathon.

---

## Why marathon training is different

The marathon tests aerobic endurance, fat oxidation, glycogen management, and mental durability in a way shorter races don't. Key adaptations needed:

- **Long runs** build mitochondria density, fat oxidation, musculoskeletal durability
- **Marathon-pace runs** teach the body to run efficiently at goal pace
- **Threshold work** raises the % of VO2max sustainable at marathon pace
- **High weekly mileage** is the single biggest predictor of marathon success

## 18-Week Marathon Plan

### Phase overview

| Phase | Weeks | Weekly km | Key focus |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Base | 1–5 | 65–85 km | Aerobic foundation, long run |
| Build 1 | 6–10 | 80–100 km | Threshold + marathon-pace work |
| Build 2 | 11–15 | 90–110 km | Long runs with M-pace sections, volume peak |
| Peak + Taper | 16–18 | Taper | Race-specific sharpening, CTL → positive TSB |

### Weekly template

```
Mon: Rest or 30 min very easy
Tue: Quality 1 (Q1) — threshold or VO2max
Wed: Easy 55–65 min
Thu: Medium-long run (14–20 km, easy) OR Quality 2 
Fri: Easy 45–55 min
Sat: Easy 50–60 min + strides
Sun: Long run
```

---

### Phase 1: Base (Weeks 1–5)

Build the aerobic platform. No pressure on quality yet. Long runs are the priority.

**Week 1**
- Thu medium-long: 14 km E
- Q (light): 40 min E + 8 strides
- Long: 24 km E
- Total: ~70 km

**Week 2**
- Thu: 15 km E
- Q1: 3 × 10 min @ T pace, 90 sec rest
- Long: 26 km E
- Total: ~75 km

**Week 3**
- Thu: 16 km E
- Q1: 20 min sustained tempo
- Long: 28 km E
- Total: ~80 km

**Week 4**
- Thu: 15 km E
- Q1: 4 × 8 min @ T pace, 90 sec rest
- Long: 29 km E + 10 strides afterward
- Total: ~82 km

**Week 5 (Recovery)**
- No quality; all easy
- Long: 20 km E
- Total: ~58 km

---

### Phase 2: Build 1 (Weeks 6–10)

Introduce marathon-pace work in the long run and VO2max to raise the ceiling.

**Week 6**
- Q1 (Tue): 3 × 10 min @ T pace, 2 min rest
- Thu medium-long: 18 km E
- Long: 24 km E + last 8 km at M pace
- Total: ~85 km

**Week 7**
- Q1 (Tue): 5 × 1000m @ I pace, 3 min jog
- Q2 (Thu): 22 km medium-long with 10 km @ M pace in the middle
- Long: 26 km E
- Total: ~88 km

**Week 8**
- Q1 (Tue): 2 × 15 min @ T pace, 2 min rest
- Thu: 18 km E
- Long: 30 km E + last 10 km @ M pace
- Total: ~90 km

**Week 9**
- Q1 (Tue): 4 × 1200m @ I pace, 3 min jog
- Q2 (Thu): 25 km medium-long E
- Long: 32 km E + last 12 km @ M pace
- Total: ~95 km

**Week 10 (Recovery)**
- Q1 only: 20 min easy tempo
- Long: 22 km E
- Total: ~65 km

---

### Phase 3: Build 2 (Weeks 11–15)

Peak volume. Long runs at their longest. M-pace sections get longer.

**Week 11**
- Q1 (Tue): 3 × 12 min @ T pace, 2 min rest
- Q2 (Thu): 20 km E + 8 km @ M pace (progression long)
- Long: 32 km — 10 km E warmup + 16 km @ M pace + 6 km E cooldown
- Total: ~100 km

**Week 12**
- Q1 (Tue): 6 × 1000m @ I pace, 3 min jog
- Thu: 20 km E
- Long: 33 km — 6 km E + 22 km @ M pace + 5 km E
- Total: ~100 km

**Week 13**
- Q1 (Tue): 25 min sustained tempo
- Q2 (Thu): 22 km progressive (E → M pace in final 8 km)
- Long: 34 km E (steady, no M pace — recovery long)
- Total: ~105 km

**Week 14 (Mini-taper / Recovery)**
- Q1 only: 3 × 10 min @ T pace
- Long: 24 km E
- Total: ~72 km

**Week 15**
- Q1 (Tue): 4 × 10 min @ T pace, 90 sec rest
- Q2 (Thu): 22 km with 12 km @ M pace
- Long: 32 km with 14 km @ M pace
- Total: ~100 km  ← Volume peak

---

### Phase 4: Peak + Taper (Weeks 16–18)

Race-specific sharpening. TSB rises toward +15 to +20 range.

**Week 16**
- Q1 (Tue): 3 × 8 min @ T pace, 90 sec rest
- Q2 (Thu): 18 km with 8 km @ M pace
- Long: 26 km — all E (no M pace this week)
- Total: ~80 km  (−20% from peak)

**Week 17 (2 weeks out)**
- Q1 (Tue): 2 × 15 min @ T pace, 2 min rest
- Thu: 16 km E with 6 km @ M pace
- Long: 22 km — 10 km E + 10 km @ M pace + 2 km E
- Total: ~65 km  (−35% from peak)

**Week 18 (Race week)**
- Mon: 35 min easy
- Tue: 25 min easy + 5 × 200m @ I pace + 6 strides
- Wed: 30 min easy
- Thu: 20 min easy + 3 × 1 km @ M pace + 4 strides
- Fri: 20 min very easy or rest
- Sat: 15–20 min very easy shakeout + 4 strides
- **Sun: Marathon race**

## Race day execution

### The pacing plan

**The golden rule:** The second half must feel achievable from mile 1.

**Km 1–5:** 10–15 sec/km conservative. There are people running too fast around you. Let them go. You will pass them later.

**Km 5–25:** Goal marathon pace. Consistent effort (not pace — effort adjusts for hills).

**Km 25–35:** The test. You are now in territory you have practiced on tired long runs. This is where the race is won or lost.

**Km 35–42.2:** Gut it out. There are no more pacing calculations. Just finish.

### Fueling protocol

| Km mark | Action |
|---------|--------|
| 0 | Start with 2 gels taken 15 min before start (or carb-load breakfast) |
| 8–10 km | Gel 1 (take at water station, wash down with water) |
| 16–18 km | Gel 2 |
| 24–26 km | Gel 3 |
| 32–34 km | Gel 4 (if you can tolerate it) |

Water at every station from km 10 onward. Don't skip stations even if you don't feel thirsty.

### Glycogen loading
- Day 3: 8–10 g CHO/kg body weight (e.g., 640–800g for 80 kg runner)
- Day 2: 8–10 g CHO/kg
- Day 1 (eve before): 6–8 g CHO/kg (easy on fiber; white rice, pasta, bread)
- Morning of race: 2–3 hr before: 200–300 g CHO (oats, white toast, banana); avoid high-fiber

### The wall
Occurs when glycogen is depleted. Prevention:
1. Adequate CTL and long-run base
2. Consistent in-race fueling (critical)
3. Conservative start pace

If you hit it: slow down immediately, take a gel, reduce to a run-walk if needed.
